Choreography 
Just as a river’s flow is directed around rocks and pebbles, choreography
shapes the way a dance flows. Choreography is the art of creating and
arranging dance movements to form a visual pattern or spectacle. The
placement of dancers, their directions, pauses, and movements are
decisions choreographers make to bring their vision to life. 
Do you know? 
The first known thinker on the idea of choreography in India is
Sage Bhārata, the author of the Nāṭyaśhāstra . More than 2000
years ago, he described a concept called ‘ Piṇḍībandhas ’, which are
group formations characterised by clustering, weaving, linking,
and moving in coordinated shapes.
Many of these ancient ideas appear in choreographies even today,
such as: 
 group formations and shapes; 
 coordinated travelling patterns; and 
 movement flow in unison or in opposition.

saṁrachanā ’ (dance creation) to refer to dance choreography.
Creating a dance piece involves imagination, planning, teamwork, and
the thoughtful use of all the elements and techniques of dance and

Concept: A clear subject, story, or theme that the dance will explore (for example,
seasons, friendships, journeys, celebrations, emotions, or socially relevant ideas) 
Movement Vocabulary: The dance style(s) used in the piece (for example,
classical, folk, contemporary, freestyle, or a combination) 
Space: Use of formations, directions, spatial levels, distances between dancers,
and patterns across the performance space 
Practice and Refinement: Rehearsing, editing, and polishing movement
sequences to create clarity, synchronisation, and expressive impact 
Style: The chosen genre of presentation (for example, theatrical, lyrical, fantasy-
based, narrative, folk-inspired, or abstract) 
Time: Decisions about rhythm, tempo, pauses, and timing that influence the mood
and pacing of the choreography 
Choreographic Tools: Devices such as repetition, contrast, canon, unison,
variation, formation changes, mirroring, and the use of props

Dance Choreography Movement patterns in choreography 
There are infinite ways in which patterns can be created through
movement. The process of creating movement patterns can include: 
 linking movements into sequences; 
 designing shapes with the body; 
 arranging dancers into formations; and 
 creating symmetry or asymmetry.
Movement patterns in choreography carry layered meanings, especially 
within folk traditions. Rooted in everyday life and collective experience, 
these patterns emerge through simple steps, repetition, and group 
formations. In Indian regional dances, such movement vocabulary 
becomes a reflection of community life like shared work, social bonds, 
and collective celebration expressed through bodies moving together 
in rhythm.
Human formations, such as pyramids, are commonly seen in several 
Indian dance forms.

Creating your choreography 
Divide yourself into four groups. Choose your
lead choregrapher. 
 First, each choreographer
creates a solo movement
portraying a concept of 
their choice (for example,

etc.). They may choose any

 Next, each lead choreographer must adapt their solo phrase into

and interaction. 
 The choreography can include aspects of all four abhinayas ,
such as: 
 expressive, stylised movements (use of hand gestures, 
animation, and mime); 
 rhythmic syllables and spoken word; 
 props; and 
 emotional expression. 
 Each solo choreographer presents their choreography, followed
by the group performance of the same. 
 Observe each group’s performance and explore how it can
change and how expression changes with group interaction 
and usage of space in a: 
 solo performance; and 
 group performance

Reflections 
Reflect on and analyse the choreography created in Activity 13.2.

and effectiveness of your work.
