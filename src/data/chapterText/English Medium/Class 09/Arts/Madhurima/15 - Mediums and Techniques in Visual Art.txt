Care, Maintenance, and Responsible Storage
of Materials 
It is important to be organised and careful while using and
storing materials. This not only ensures that they can be shared with
others but also preserves the vibrancy and longevity of artworks.
Here are some useful practices that can be followed: 
Source consciously 
 Choose non-toxic and sustainable materials as much as possible. 
 Reuse paper and experiment with recyclable materials. 
Handle and maintain with care 
 Clean brushes immediately after use. Wash them with soap and
rinse thoroughly. Shape their bristles and dry them horizontally or
upright (with bristles facing up). 
 Close paint tubes tightly and properly, and replace the cap of
markers and ink bottles after using to prevent drying. 
 Do not touch artworks with dirty or oily hands. 
 Dispose of inks, acrylics, and chemical waste like turpentine
responsibly.

Visit a stationery store or an art supplies store to see how paper, books,

 Store paper, canvas, and drawings in dry, flat spaces.

tools safely in a box at a safe space, away from young children,

Śholāpith is a crafts tradition practised in
West Bengal and Odisha. A similar tradition
practised in Tamil Nadu is called Neṭṭī . Artists
of this tradition extract a milky-white spongy

carved decorative objects. Traditionally, 
śholāpith was used to craft garlands, headgear
for brides and grooms, as well as idols of
deities, and as decorations during festivities.
The craft is practised by artisans called 
malakars (garland-makers), although they
craft much more than garlands these days.

Explore Mediums with Colour Theory 
The Chitra-sūtra of the Viṣhṇu-dharmottara Purāṇa elaborates on every
aspect of sourcing, extracting, preparing, and using colours. It lists the
basic colours as white, yellow, red, blue, and black . When these are
mixed, they produce innumerable colours.
The text also mentions that the secondary colours yield many variations
of tints and shades by adding varying amounts of white and black
respectively.

Perspective Techniques 
Perspective is a drawing technique that is used to show the
three-dimensionality or spatial depth on a two-dimensional surface.
There are two kinds of perspective techniques, which are
explained below. 
Linear perspective 
This is the method where lines that are parallel in reality are drawn to
converge towards a vanishing point on the horizon. For example, the
parallel lines of a corridor are drawn to converge in the distance, which
creates the illusion of spatial depth (Fig. 15.8).
